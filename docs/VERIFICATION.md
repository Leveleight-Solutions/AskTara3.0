The initial actual-model runs exposed real defects, which were fixed before the affected scenarios were replayed:

- A valid Ubud stay could be rejected because the destination catalogue recognised the broader Bali region. Literal city/region qualifiers and named stays now take precedence while retaining source and night-count validation.
- Absolute “extend/lengthen/shorten” corrections could be read as part of a destination name. A unique qualified locality now accepts its short name and applies the supplied nights and departure together; incorrect night counts are still rejected.
- Multiple-stop briefs could lose the first stay length or final departure date. Date evidence is scoped to the relevant stop, and corrections preserve the other stops and trip boundaries.
- “No children” could incorrectly negate a later Australian-passport declaration. Passport negation is now scoped to the identity statement; plural declarations work, while mixed/unknown passports remain unresolved.
- “Whole trip, not per day” could clear a valid budget. Budget-basis negation, supported currencies, adjacent forms such as `AUD3000`, and currency-before-label wording now have regressions.
- Changing to one-way travel clears a previously chosen return mode. Origin/departure/mode corrections flag affected transport services for review and invalidate cached supplier quotes; cabin changes review flights.
- Reapplying a cruise could overwrite outbound flight intent, append cruise days after later land dates, or erase manual transfers. Stable source-day IDs preserve edits through deletion, reordering and immediate port/date changes. A successful editor save receives those assigned IDs.
- Real AI regeneration treated a reviewed sea day as an unresolved gap. Cruise rows are now retained by the application while AI researches the land days. Reviewed ports, sea days, early exit, full fare and manual transfers remain intact after regeneration and later preference edits. Ambiguous mixed dates are rejected without replacing the saved plan.
- Cruise approval and chat now account for a voyage ending with sea days after its last port. The lightweight date check still allows 366-day and undated manual schedules; it does not inherit AI generation limits.
- An unsupported citation caused a family plan to fail. One fresh researched repair is now allowed within the original request deadline, with full source, route and privacy validation; repeated invalid evidence still fails without saving a partial plan.

The separate live supplier-party matrix passed three cases: Orlando hotel pricing for two adults plus children aged six and ten, Kathmandu hotel pricing for five adults, and solo Lisbon hotel plus actual one-way flight search/selection. It returned 205, 3 and 202 sandbox hotel quotes respectively, and 30 one-way flight quotes. Kathmandu explicitly reported incomplete inventory. Every created workspace was deleted. These searches request one room; automatic multiroom allocation is not implemented. Family flight searches explicitly require a reviewed manual quote rather than silently omitting children.

Current limits remain explicit in the testing guide: sandbox prices are not confirmed reservations, future operating hours and availability require verification, AI plans have a 35-calendar-day limit, and a finite matrix cannot establish every possible trip or future model response. These checks establish local behaviour, not a new production deployment.
